 HISTORY
 This is a 36-year-old right-hand-dominant male warehouse logistics coordinator referred for evaluation of persistent right lower
 extremity radicular pain following a rear-end motor vehicle collision on 02/11/2024. He was evaluated in the emergency
 department on the date of injury with negative radiographs. Symptoms have persisted for five weeks despite naproxen, gabapentin,
 methocarbamol, and six sessions of physical therapy.
 He describes low back pain of 5/10 and right leg pain of 7/10, the leg pain now dominant. Pain radiates from the right buttock
 through the posterolateral thigh and calf into the dorsum of the right foot. He reports numbness over the dorsum of the right foot
 and a sense that the right foot 'catches' when he is tired. He denies left leg symptoms, denies bowel or bladder dysfunction, and
 denies saddle anesthesia.
 He remains off work. He rates his function as markedly limited and reports inability to sit through a full meal or drive more than 20
 minutes.

 REVIEW OF PRIOR RECORDS
 I have personally reviewed the ED records of 02/11/2024, the lumbar radiographs of the same date, and the MRI lumbar spine of
 03/06/2024. I have also reviewed the cervical spine MRI dated 03/2024 which demonstrates no significant cord compression.
 Physical therapy notes reviewed.

 EXAMINATION
 Gait antalgic on the right. Heel walking on the right is weak; toe walking intact. Lumbar flexion to 50 degrees, extension to 15
 degrees with reproduction of leg pain.
 Motor: Right EHL 4/5. Right tibialis anterior 4+/5. Right gastrocnemius 5/5. Left lower extremity 5/5 throughout. Hip abduction
 symmetric.
 Sensory: Diminished pinprick right L5 dermatome, dorsum of foot and lateral calf.
 Reflexes: Patellar 2+ bilaterally. Achilles 2+ left, 1+ right.
 Provocative: Right straight leg raise positive at 35 degrees reproducing leg pain. Contralateral SLR negative. FABER negative
 bilaterally. No hip joint pain with internal rotation.

 ASSESSMENT
 Right L5 radiculopathy secondary to an L4-L5 right paracentral disc protrusion, symptomatic and correlating with imaging.
 Underlying multilevel degenerative disc disease is present and is chronic; in my opinion the acute focal protrusion and the onset of
 radicular symptoms are temporally related to the collision of 02/11/2024, in a spine with pre-existing degenerative change that was
 asymptomatic prior to the event.

 PLAN
 1. Continue physical therapy, extend authorization by 4 weeks.
 2. Referral to interventional pain management for right L5 transforaminal epidural steroid injection.
 3. Continue gabapentin, titrate to 300 mg TID.
 4. Repeat evaluation in 6 weeks. If radicular pain and EHL weakness persist despite injection, would discuss right L4-L5
 microdiscectomy.
 5. Work status: remains off work.
