 PREOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS

 POSTOPERATIVE DIAGNOSIS
 Same, confirmed intraoperatively.

 PROCEDURE PERFORMED
 Right L4-L5 hemilaminotomy with microdiscectomy and decompression of the right L5 nerve root (CPT 63030).

 INDICATIONS
 The patient is a 37-year-old male with a right L4-L5 disc protrusion causing right L5 radiculopathy with motor deficit, which has
 failed a course of physical therapy, oral neuropathic and anti-inflammatory medication, and one transforaminal epidural steroid
 injection. Symptoms have persisted for approximately seven months. Risks, benefits, and alternatives were reviewed and informed
 consent obtained.

 DESCRIPTION OF PROCEDURE
 The patient was brought to the operating room and general anesthesia was induced without difficulty. He was turned prone onto a
 Wilson frame with all pressure points padded. A surgical time-out was performed confirming patient identity, procedure, and
 correct operative level and laterality.
 The lumbar region was prepped and draped in the usual sterile fashion. A localizing radiograph confirmed the L4-L5 interspace. A
 midline incision was made and carried through subcutaneous tissue. Subperiosteal dissection was carried out on the right side
 exposing the L4 lamina and L4-L5 interlaminar space. A retractor was placed and a repeat radiograph confirmed the level.
 Using the operating microscope, a hemilaminotomy of the inferior margin of the right L4 lamina was performed with a high-speed
 burr and Kerrison rongeurs. The ligamentum flavum was incised and removed. The thecal sac and traversing right L5 nerve root
 were identified. The nerve root was noted to be tented and erythematous over a firm extruded disc fragment in the right lateral
 recess.
 The nerve root was gently retracted medially. The fragment was delivered with a pituitary rongeur and additional loose
 degenerative nucleus pulposus was removed from the disc space. The lateral recess and foramen were then probed and found to be
 widely decompressed. The nerve root was noted to be freely mobile.
 Hemostasis was obtained with bipolar cautery and thrombin-soaked Gelfoam. The wound was irrigated with antibiotic solution.
 The fascia was closed with 0-Vicryl, subcutaneous tissue with 2-0 Vicryl, and skin with a running subcuticular 4-0 Monocryl. A
 sterile dressing was applied. All counts were correct. The patient was awakened, extubated, and transferred to the post-anesthesia
 care unit in stable condition.
