 CHIEF COMPLAINT
 Neck and low back pain following motor vehicle collision.

 HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS
 The patient is a 36-year-old male with no significant past medical history who presents by EMS following a motor vehicle collision
 that occurred earlier this evening. He reports he was stopped at a traffic signal when his vehicle was struck from behind by a pickup
 truck. He was restrained by a three-point harness. Airbags did not deploy. He denies striking his head, denies loss of consciousness,
 and denies any period of confusion.
 He describes immediate onset of posterior neck pain, non-radiating, rated 6/10, and low back pain rated 8/10 which radiates into the
 right buttock and down the posterior right thigh to approximately the level of the knee. He describes an associated 'electric'
 sensation with certain movements. He denies bowel or bladder incontinence, denies saddle anesthesia, denies fever, and denies
 prior similar symptoms. He states he has never had back problems before and has never been treated for back pain.

 PAST MEDICAL HISTORY
 Unremarkable per patient report. No prior surgeries. No chronic medications.

 ALLERGIES
 Codeine - rash.

 PHYSICAL EXAMINATION
 General: Well-developed male, uncomfortable but in no acute distress.
 HEENT: Normocephalic, atraumatic. No scalp hematoma. EOMI, PERRL.
 Neck: Midline cervical tenderness at C5-C6. No step-off, no crepitus. Range of motion reduced in all planes secondary to pain and
 guarding.
 Chest/CV: Clear to auscultation. Regular rate and rhythm. No chest wall tenderness, no seatbelt sign.
 Abdomen: Soft, non-distended, non-tender.
 Back: Lumbar paraspinal muscle spasm palpable bilaterally, more pronounced on the right. Tenderness at L4-L5 and L5-S1 levels.
 No midline step-off.
 Neuro: Cranial nerves II-XII intact. Motor 5/5 throughout except right hip flexion and great toe extension limited to 4+/5,
 effort-limited by pain. Sensation intact. Deep tendon reflexes 2+ and symmetric at patella and Achilles. Straight leg raise positive
 on the right at approximately 45 degrees, negative on the left.

 MEDICAL DECISION MAKING
 Given midline cervical tenderness with a distracting injury, NEXUS criteria are not satisfied and CT cervical spine was obtained.
 Lumbar radiographs obtained given focal tenderness and radicular complaints. Imaging reviewed independently by me and is
 without acute traumatic finding, as detailed in the radiology reports. Neurologic examination is non-focal apart from pain-limited
 effort. No red flag features for cauda equina.
 Working impression is acute cervical strain and acute lumbosacral strain with right-sided radicular symptoms following motor
 vehicle collision. Discussed conservative management, red flag return precautions, and outpatient follow-up.
